The conjecture concerns simple connected regular graphs of order at least two. Write θ for the fewest cliques partitioning the vertices and γₑ for the edge domination number. The exact claim is θ ≤ (4/3)γₑ + 1. GraphCalc 2.0.0 computes θ by optimally coloring the complement and counting nonempty colors. Its minimum-maximal-matching wrapper returns the domination number of the line graph. Lean uses both source definitions, and checks the independently defined minimum size of a maximal matching on the counterexample family. ## Sharp replacement bounds

On every positive-degree regular graph, **θ ≤ 2γₑ**. For a minimum edge-dominating set D, the set S of its endpoints is a vertex cover of size at most 2|D|. Every vertex outside S has all d neighbors in S, and each vertex of S receives at most d of these incidences. Double-counting incidences from each subset of the outside vertices gives Hall's condition. Match every outside vertex into S, then take the matched pairs and remaining singleton vertices of S as the clique partition. Connectedness and nontriviality ensure positive degree on the catalogue domain.

On every bipartite d-regular graph with d > 0, the stronger bound is **dθ ≤ (2d−1)γₑ**. Both sides of the bipartition are vertex covers, so the same Hall argument gives 2θ ≤ |V|. Each selected edge meets at most 2d−1 edges, counting itself once. Thus |E| ≤ (2d−1)γₑ. The degree sum gives d|V| = 2|E|.

For each d ≥ 2, take two copies of K_{d,d−1} and join their larger parts by a perfect matching between the copies. The result is connected, bipartite and d-regular, of order 4d−2. The d joining edges form a dominating induced matching. The graph is triangle-free, so the published row 1078 minimum-degree lower bound forces both edge optima to equal d. A perfect matching of the whole graph pairs d−1 vertices internally in each copy and then pairs the remaining two vertices. Each bipartition side has 2d−1 vertices, so θ = 2d−1.

This attains the degree-dependent coefficient for every d ≥ 2. As d increases, the ratio θ/γₑ approaches two. Lean also proves that **for every rational c < 2 and every fixed rational A**, some connected regular bipartite member has θ > cγₑ + A. In the sources, n = d−2. The main theorems are `catalogue_factor_two`, `bipartite_degree_bound`, `family`, `fixed_degree_equality`, `sharp_affine` and `row1957_false`.

## Prior work and released witnesses

The factor-two upper bound is a consequence of established matching theory. Caro, Davila and Pepper showed that the independence number α is at most the maximum matching size ν on positive-degree regular graphs. The matched edges of a maximum matching together with unmatched singletons give θ ≤ |V|−ν ≤ |V|−α. The final quantity is the vertex cover number, which is at most twice the size of any edge-dominating set. The package gives its own Hall proof. The degree-dependent bound uses elementary edge counting, and the equality construction uses a dominating induced matching, a standard extremal mechanism. The exact application and witnesses are certified here. Historical priority for the sharp coefficient in this formulation is unverified. [Caro, Davila and Pepper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.09093) · [Dominating induced matchings and other graph parameters](https://doi.org/10.1080/09728600.2024.2342310) · [Baste et al. on minimum maximal matchings in regular graphs](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.disc.2020.112243)

Three bundled files were checked byte-for-byte against the public repository. Row 1121 supplies the Hall injection, its clique coloring and the exact definition of θ. Row 1078 supplies the line-graph optimization, triangle-free degree lower bound and its comparison with minimum maximal matching. Its bundled matching basis supplies the matched endpoint representation. The proof that edge domination yields an endpoint vertex cover adapts the counting in row 574.
