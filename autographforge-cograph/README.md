# Radius and the Slater number of a connected cograph

**Row 143 holds as written.** In a connected cograph of order at least two, the radius and Slater number both equal 1 if there is a universal vertex, and both equal 2 otherwise. In the regular case the diameter has that same value. This proves row 818 on the numerical diameter domain. Row 1128 follows because a sum of three pairwise distances is at most three diameters.

## Argument and attribution

These are elementary consequences of Seinsche’s 1974 characterization and the classical Slater degree bound. A connected induced-$P_4$-free graph of order at least two has disconnected complement. Partition its vertices into one complement component and the remaining components. Every vertex on either side is adjacent to every vertex on the other. This gives distance at most two and a dominating edge. Equivalently, the neighborhoods of the ends of that edge cover all vertices. [Seinsche, *On a property of the class of n-colorable graphs*](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/009589567490063X) · [Corneil, Lerchs and Stewart Burlingham, *Complement reducible graphs*](https://doi.org/10.1016/0166-218X%2881%2990013-5) · [Gentner and Rautenbach, *Some Comments on the Slater Number*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1608.04560)

For the degree calculation, choose a maximum-degree vertex $x$ and a vertex $y$ outside its complement component. Their two degrees sum to at least the order because their neighborhoods cover the graph. The two largest degrees sum to at least as much, including when the maximum is tied, so the Slater number is at most two. A single degree satisfies the Slater test exactly when its vertex is universal. Radius one has the same characterization. In a regular graph with a universal vertex every vertex is universal. Otherwise all distances are at most two and the Slater number is two. The complete graph $K_2$ illustrates GraphCalc’s empty-triple convention: its radius, diameter and Slater number are one and its triameter is zero.

## Definitions, implementation and reuse

`Cograph` means there is no injection from four ordered vertices preserving adjacency in both directions with a four-vertex path. The file `Proof137.lean` includes the structural proof from the published Graffiti.pc #137 module. The excerpt omits the path-cover material and uses explicit natural-number arithmetic at the final cardinality contradiction. The theorem `graphCalc_cograph_iff` proves that this induced-path predicate satisfies every branch of GraphCalc’s recursive recognition algorithm. An induced four-vertex path is connected, and its complement is connected in the order $2,0,3,1$. Thus a forbidden path sits within a single component of either graph. In the complement-component branch, the recursive test induces the **original** graph on that component’s vertex set. [Published #137 proof](https://github.com/vjdad4m/maths/blob/main/graffiti-137/Proof137.lean) · [GraphCalc cograph implementation](https://github.com/RandyRDavila/GraphCalc/blob/ce66ac7390d945885e33f372e29c896fa32e9bc4/src/graphcalc/graphs/core/basics.py#L1126-L1194)

The Lean definitions of sorted degrees and the Slater number come from #33. The radius, diameter and distinct-triple triameter reuse the #501/#764 definitions. The handling of undefined distances follows #821. GraphCalc tests nontriviality by order at least two and regularity by equality of all degrees. Its Slater routine sorts degrees in descending order and searches for the least prefix whose sum plus its length reaches the order. [Row 33](https://github.com/vjdad4m/maths/blob/main/autographforge-33/Lollipop.lean) · [Rows 501/764](https://github.com/vjdad4m/maths/blob/main/autographforge-eulerian-chain/Chain.lean) · [Row 821](https://github.com/vjdad4m/maths/blob/main/autographforge-821/ForestSlater.lean) · [GraphCalc Slater implementation](https://github.com/RandyRDavila/GraphCalc/blob/ce66ac7390d945885e33f372e29c896fa32e9bc4/src/graphcalc/graphs/invariants/degree.py#L227-L365)

GraphCalc calls NetworkX for radius and diameter. Both raise on disconnected graphs of order at least two. GraphCalc’s triameter raises `ValueError` on disconnected inputs and starts the maximum over unordered triples of distinct vertices at zero. The CEGIS table masks undefined numerical cells. The separate wrapper returns positive infinity for disconnected radius and diameter. Row 143 already requires connectivity. Two isolated vertices give row 818 a wrapper value of infinity and a Slater number of two. The wrapper provides no triameter implementation, so the source supplies no analogous infinity-valued comparison for row 1128. ## Replay

The complete local Lean source is `Proof137.lean` and `CographRadius.lean`. With Lean 4.19.0 on PATH and a prepared Mathlib v4.19.0 checkout at commit `c44e0c8ee63ca166450922a373c7409c5d26b00b`, run from this directory:

```bash
MATHLIB_DIR=/absolute/path/to/mathlib4 bash replay.sh
```

The script compiles the local dependency in a fresh directory, checks both sources with warnings as errors and checks the axiom output of the catalogue results and structural lemmas. The only reported axioms are `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`.

With GraphCalc 2.0.0 and NetworkX 3.5 installed, run `python check_source.py /absolute/path/to/pinned/AutoGraphForge`. The script checks the physical row texts, compares the cograph recognizer with induced-four-vertex enumeration on all 1,253 atlas graphs, and exercises the numeric rows on all 143 qualifying connected cographs, including the 10 regular ones. It also exercises the disconnected exceptions and the wrapper’s infinity convention.
