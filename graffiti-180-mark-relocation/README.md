# Graffiti.pc #180, degree-two marks at a saturated center

This Lean-checked partial result continues the twenty-module opposite-gain package. The full conjecture remains open. All twenty embedded predecessor Lean sources were compared with public commit 2ebf7c4ce1870299eeb82745fd9387a88e50320f and matched byte for byte.

For a fixed root r, distance parity P and independent set A, K is the set of independent marks in P together with r when marked. The allowance is W+n−1−|A|−|P|, where W is the maximum number of eligible leaves in a spanning tree. At a saturated center a in A∩P one has deg(a)=allowance+2.

* MarkDeletion180 proves the exact effect of deleting a nonroot mark. Deleting a selected-parity mark raises the allowance by one. Deleting an off-parity mark preserves it exactly when that mark is a leaf in every original weighted optimum.
* SaturatedMoves180 proves that any independent replacement of at least the original cardinality, keeping a protected with a smaller eligible set, preserves the allowance and optimum. It specializes this to a single admissible move into the selected parity.
* LowMasks180 and LowPins180 establish that independent pins of degree at most two may be omitted from the protected bipartization test. On any terminal region admitting a free deletion, the equivalence holds separately for each prescribed terminal sign; empty masks are included. Each active region has a free vertex since its center has a neighbor there and the pins are independent.
* PoolRepair180 transfers the marking into its original parity whenever sufficiently many independent available vertices exist. In any failed rank-two single-tip case, for every retained set J between the high pins and all pins, the number of nonroot selected-parity candidates outside J and its neighborhood and distinct from the other tip is at most 2(|A|−|J|)−2.
* MissedPorts180 bounds the number of unmarked endpoints of selected-parity edges missing N(a) by the number of off-parity nonroot independent marks. The proof injects the endpoints into shortest predecessors. Each additional independent, opposite-parity neighbor retained at a pays for deleting one such endpoint.
* ParityCover180 gives an exact test for using the original distance colors. Choose an independent subset J of the opposite-parity neighbors of a. Choose a vertex cover S outside N(a) of all selected-parity edges which miss that neighborhood, using only selected-parity vertices outside K. The deletion budget suffices exactly when such J,S exist with |J|≥|S|+2. If there is a single missed unmarked endpoint this reduces to existence of three independent opposite-parity neighbors. This criterion concerns fixed distance colors; a general bipartite witness may recolor.
* SoleMark180 combines these results. In any failed single-tip transversal branch with exactly one off-parity nonroot mark v, deleting v raises the allowance from two to three, some original maximizing tree has v internal, the available-vertex pool for J=K is empty, the missed-endpoint set is a singleton, and at most two mutually independent opposite-parity neighbors can be kept. This applies both when one terminal mask is empty and when both are nonempty and disjoint.
* TightUnique180 proves that on the previously checked nine-vertex prime example (r=1, even parity, center 0), the only tight independent marking containing the center is {0,1,2,3}. Both 2 and 3 have degree two. The example satisfies the protected test; its masks overlap.

* PendantMark180 certifies an eleven-vertex prime graph with a tight mixed marking. Add the path 6–9–10 to the nine-vertex example. At root 1 and even parity, the only tight independent sets containing 0 are {0,1,2,3,9} and {0,1,2,3,10}. The unique off-parity nonroot mark 9 has degree two. Deleting it raises the allowance to three, while moving it to 10 preserves two. Both connected active regions have a common sign, the root has no dominating edge, deleting 9 disconnects the graph, and the ordinary #180 inequality holds. This supplies an explicit example in which relocation succeeds and deletion fails; the example has a cut vertex.

All theorem hypotheses are in the Lean sources. No minimum-counterexample or graph census assumption is needed.
