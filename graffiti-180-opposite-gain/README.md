# Graffiti.pc 180. Two active components and the deletion allowance

This private package proves sufficient conditions for the stronger protected test used in the prime-substitution reduction of Graffiti.pc 180. Throughout, G is a finite simple connected graph, r is the root, P is one of its two distance parities, and A is independent. The pins are K = A ∩ (P ∪ {r}). An eligible vertex lies in A ∪ P, with r eligible only when r belongs to both A and P. Let W be the largest number of eligible leaves in a spanning tree. The exact deletion allowance is B = W + |V| − (1 + |A| + |P|). A protected repair deletes at most B vertices outside K to make G bipartite.

The transversal is a pair {a,b}. The center a is the sole protected member and differs from the root. The earlier rigidity theorem proves that any failed marking has degree(a) = 4 and B = 2. In the two-component statements there are two separated active regions C and D in the bipartite remainder. Each has a path joining opposite-colored neighbors of a. The inactive part has no neighbor of a. For each region its terminal mask records the possible relative colors of a and b after one deletion outside K. The prior exact theorem identifies the intersection of these two masks with a protected repair at allowance two.

## Results

**Exact obstruction at allowance two.** In two connected active regions with nonempty disjoint terminal masks, `tight_opposite_has_degree_two` proves that B = 2 forces a nonroot member of A of graph degree exactly two. Equivalently, `connected_opposite_gain` proves B ≥ 3 whenever no nonroot independent mark has degree two. Degree-one marks are allowed. The second tip may have any root distance and any marking consistent with being unprotected. The result applies in particular to graphs of minimum degree at least three.

* `masks_force_three` proves B ≥ 3 if the two masks have empty intersection and any one of the following conditions holds. It allows an individual mask to be empty.
  1. A ⊆ P ∪ {r}.
  2. Every edge with both ends in P occurs in the distance layer of a.
  3. Each nonroot mark in A outside P has degree at least three, and there is no edge inside P exactly two layers beyond a.
  4. No nonroot vertex of A has degree two, and either b lies outside A ∪ P or its root distance is different from both d(a)+1 and d(a)+2.
* `opposite_forces_three` is specific to disjoint **nonempty** masks. If no nonroot member of A has degree two, opposite forced colors imply B ≥ 3 whenever d(b) ≠ d(a)+2. No bound on eccentricity or on the degrees of vertices outside A is required. Here d(v) denotes distance from r. `opposite_contacts` proves that each active region then contains at least two neighbors of b, hence degree(b) ≥ 4.
* `solo_scope` proves a protected repair under any of conditions 1–3 for a single-protected nonroot transversal marking, regardless of the number of active components or of the initial allowance. In particular minimum degree three and eccentricity(r) ≤ d(a)+1 suffice.
* `remote_obstruction` describes a necessary witness at every failed single-protected nonroot marking. A selected-parity edge misses the whole neighborhood of a and lies outside its distance layer. One endpoint x is outside A. All its shortest predecessors lie in A. There is a nonroot predecessor z outside P. Either degree(z) ≤ 2, or d(x) = d(a)+2 and z and a have a common neighbor.
* `ExampleNine.sharp_gain` applies the new implication to the frozen nine-vertex prime graph at root 6. Its two incompatible signs and the parity-contained independent marking give B ≥ 3, and the previously certified exact value is 3. This example establishes sharpness of the parity-contained clause. `compatible_regressions` checks the eight-vertex recoloring example and the other nine-vertex graph at allowance two through the new graph-wide repair theorem.

## Why the two-region argument works

The accounting lemma `parent_gain_with_root` keeps track of additional children with parents outside both marks and branching at nonroot marked vertices. It also counts excess degree at a singly marked root. A full star can be retained at an independently marked center, and two nonroot independent centers with disjoint neighborhoods can retain both stars. Consequently, at equality every other nonroot independent mark of degree at least three has a neighbor in common with a.

An edge outside both marks joins equal distance layers. Giving one endpoint the other as parent, while all other vertices retain shortest-path parents, produces a tree with a single exceptional edge. Its exact degree sum gives one extra unit in `layer_bonus` beyond the full singly marked layer bound. Combining this with the bipartiteness of the center neighborhood proves that a saturated center has two nonadjacent opposite-parity neighbors. If each edge inside P meets its neighborhood, delete every neighbor of the center except those two. Original distance parity colors the remaining graph. Failure therefore implies the remote edge described above.

When its predecessors have degree at least three, the remote edge lies two layers beyond a. An independently marked predecessor z has a common horizontal neighbor w with a. Its edge to z occupies the only possible opposite-parity position at w outside the neighborhood of a. A predecessor of w must therefore be a second neighbor of a, one layer below a. If the remote edge and its marked predecessors stay in the remainder, it would place three distinct neighbors of a in one region. Each region already has exactly two. The proof treats separately an independently marked second endpoint and an endpoint outside A. In the latter case a shared predecessor cannot have two such successors.

For disjoint nonempty masks, a region with at most one neighbor of b could accommodate either relative color by deleting the appropriate neighbor of a. Both regions must therefore supply at least two neighbors of b. If b is independently marked one layer beyond a, all its lower neighbors are horizontal neighbors of a, of which there are at most two. It has at most one upper neighbor. This excludes that exceptional location.

The final connected-wing argument handles b two layers beyond a. Pick a predecessor of b. It is independently marked and has a nonroot predecessor of its own, so its degree is at least two. If no nonroot independent mark has degree two, it forces a horizontal neighbor of a and a lower neighbor of a in the same wing. Those two vertices use both available positions. At the layer of b, every vertex of this wing must be a graph leaf. Any other independently marked vertex there would require a third port one layer higher. A vertex outside A there would share the predecessor of b and violate the unique-successor bound. The connected wing therefore has just one neighbor of b. This contradicts the two contacts forced by genuinely opposite masks. The layer-cut lemma explicitly allows leaf vertices at the separating layer.

## Remaining cases

The unrestricted single-protected case and Graffiti.pc 180 remain open in this work. For two connected active regions with disjoint nonempty masks at allowance two, the remaining case requires a nonroot independent mark of degree exactly two. Condition 1 also forces a nonroot mark outside P in any such failure. That mark and the degree-two mark may be different vertices. Conditions 2–3 eliminate further instances. The intermediate distance exception applies when the regions may be disconnected. Empty individual masks require the more general sufficient conditions in `masks_force_three`. The full single-component and double-protected cases remain outside these conclusions.
