# Graffiti.pc #189: adjacent true-twin cut with exactly two shores, order at least 14

Assume a connected finite simple graph remains connected after any single vertex deletion, has at most six vertices at even distance from any vertex, and has at most one vertex of degree below five. If an adjacent pair of true twins separates it into exactly two components and the graph has order at least 14, then the entire original graph has a spanning path.

The established twin bound limits these orders to 14–17. For either shore, classify vertices by distance parity from the adjacent twins. If each shore has at least four odd-depth vertices, a port spends at most one of its five positive-even slots locally. In particular deleting the at-most-one local distance-two vertices would isolate any remaining local vertices beyond distance two, so all ports miss at most one vertex in their shore. A rooted Ore argument with two possible roots constructs each shore's spanning path.

If one shore has fewer than four odd-depth vertices, it has two or three and the other shore has at least four. The smaller-odd shore still uses the near-universal argument. The other is either of order at most six or has order seven or eight (its opposing even-depth vertex sees both twins plus its even-depth vertices). For a high-degree port on that larger shore, a vertex farther than two requires two local distance-two vertices. There can only be one vertex farther than two because all such vertices have low degree. The only remaining possible failure of the high-nonedge Ore bound is two nonadjacent ports of internal degree three on an order-seven shore. But then their common far vertex needs two neighbors, both in the local distance-two set from either port. That set already includes the opposing port, which is not adjacent to the far vertex. This excludes the failure. Finally join opposite rooted routes through the original edge between the two twins.
