# Graffiti.pc #189: two adjacent shores beyond the true-twin case

Both results are for an adjacent actual separating pair with two given connected components covering every noncut vertex. At most six vertices have even distance from any vertex, including the vertex itself.

* If each shore has order at most eight, at least four vertices of equal odd distance from the cut terminals, and at least two common ports, there is a Hamiltonian path of the original graph. The usual degree-five hypothesis (at most one low vertex) and connectivity after a single deletion are required. The terminals need not be true twins. Each common port spends four positive-even slots across the cut, so the single possible local distance-two vertex cannot separate it from a farther local vertex. Common ports are therefore near-universal; other high vertices have at least four neighbors on their own shore. Rooted Ore gives paths and the original cut edge joins them.
* If one shore consists of precisely four vertices and all four are adjacent to both terminals, the entire graph has at most sixteen vertices, regardless of degrees or connectivity after a single deletion. If an exclusive port exists across the cut, the four common vertices and the opposite terminal exhaust its positive-even allowance and make it universally adjacent in its shore. At most five local vertices are ports of its preferred terminal and at most five are not. When there is no exclusive port, the twins' odd and even quotas give the same ten-vertex bound for the other shore.
