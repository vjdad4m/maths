# Adjacent singleton + twelve, written explanation of the Lean-checked result

After the strict six-profile arithmetic and application of the four-even nonadjacent separator, proceed up to swapping a,b on the following two profiles. Profile classes are EA, EB, EC, OA, OB, OC in the existing Lean order. Write s for the singleton, x for the unique A-exclusive port, y/z for B-exclusive actual ports if present. Every D vertex is high (at least degree five), because s has degree two.

## (3,4,0,1,2,2)

EA consists of depth-two vertices adjacent to x. Every OC member has balanced depth 1 or 3: if balanced depth≥5, its a-shortest predecessor has smaller a-distance even and ≥4, with a≤b, hence EA (impossible by unique OA) or EC (empty). Any EB vertex of depth≥4 can only see another EB or an OB vertex which is not a B-port. That gives at most 3+1=4 neighbors, against its degree. Thus EB all have depth two.

* If one actual B-port y, it touches all four EB. An x-y edge would give x four positive distance-two EB vertices plus b and s. So x-y is absent; deleting x,y separates any dark vertex from a,b,s via the fact that no dark vertex links to a balanced common port (that would have type EC).
* If two actual B-ports y,z and no common port, both OC balanced vertices are at depth3 and have an EA neighbor from an a-shortest walk. Thus x sees both at distance2, as well as b and s. An x-y edge would lead to at least two EB distance-two visits: y needs degree five but has no eligible neighbors other than b,x,z,EB. The potential x-z edge is ruled out identically. Then x can only have neighbors a and EA (4 total), impossible.
* If two actual B-ports and at least one common vertex c, then c can only have neighbors a,b and other ports in D. Of x,y,z it must hit at least two (and when only one common, all three). But c cannot hit y and z together: then it sees four EB plus s, and if c misses x it sees x via a; if c hits x it also sees the three EA via x. So there are exactly two common vertices. They are adjacent, touch x, and each touches exactly one of y/z. For either common vertex c, the three EA, s, and the missed B-port exhaust its five positive even slots. Thus its B-port neighbor y hits zero EB. Degree five at y forces adjacency to b,x,the other B-port and both commons, so both common vertices chose y. Consequently z hits all four EB; as before x-z must be absent. Removing x,z separates EA/EB from the remaining graph (y cannot hit EA by strictness, and hits no EB; common ports cannot hit dark).

## (3,3,1,1,1,3)

The original terminals have degree at least five with just two neighbors not in D, so at least two balanced-odd vertices are common ports. The unique exclusive ports x,y cannot touch (strict existing lemma). EA and EB have distances (2,3) and (3,2), respectively, and attach to their own exclusive port only. Each noncommon OC vertex w has (3,3): if it is deeper, the first two predecessors along an a-shortest path have distance parity even then odd and are no farther from a than b, by the adjacency of a,b and balanced distances of w. The even step cannot be strict-A even, so is balanced. The odd step cannot be strict-A odd (x is the only one and has depth1) and would force a second deep balanced odd, against at least two common ports out of OC=3. Green label v below is the sole EC.

If exactly two commons c,d, then high degree forces each to touch the other, v and exactly one of x,y. It cannot touch both exclusives (then all 6 EA/EB are at distance two). The dark odd w cannot touch any port by its distance (3,3). If c selects x, it sees EA3, s and y at distance2; as c-v, this prohibits v's EB neighbors. Similarly any common selecting y prohibits v-EA. If both select x, y must hit v for degree five, since its only other eligible neighbors are b and EB3. The five distances two of y already contain a,s,c,d,w. For w take a b-shortest predecessor; its b-distance2 and its a-distance2 or3, so predecessor is v (which y hits) or EB (which y hits). Thus v has no EA neighbors. Reverse colors for the remaining case. Hence W=EA∪EB∪{w} has induced minimum degree ≥4, since any vertex of W has at most one neighbor outside it; for w that optional neighbor is v, and for any EA/EB vertex it is its own exclusive port. A terminal-preserving Ore path through W from any EA to any EB glues to x–W–y–b–s–a–c–d–v.

If three commons K, their induced graph is complete: a common who hits x already sees EA3,s,y, so must hit every other common, else sixth distance2 via a. Analogously for selecting y; with neither, degree≥5 forces adjacency to both other commons and v. If v doesn't touch K, removing x,y separates W=EA∪EB∪{v} from the old exterior. If v touches K and hits at least two in EA∪EB, all six high vertices have induced degree at least4 in W. Complete the high clique under terminal Ore, insert v between two of its neighbors, and choose one endpoint in each color, avoiding these two neighbors as the endpoint pair. Glue x–W–y–b–s–a–K. If v hits at most one EA∪EB, set W=EA∪EB and choose exception l equal to that possible lone neighbor, or arbitrary when absent. Five nonexception W vertices have induced degree at least4 and l at least3. The same terminal closure and insertion on W with l the exception permits x–W–y–b–s–a–K–v, selecting K's order with last vertex adjacent to v. The corresponding cases are certified in the complete Lean module.
