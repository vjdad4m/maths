# Graffiti.pc #189: distance-three two-cut frontier at degree six

Strict graph-side reductions, not a complete order-20 theorem:

- In any actual two-shore cut with terminal distance at least three,
  neither cut terminal can be the exceptional vertex of degree below
  six (`cut_terminal_high_distance_three`). There is no size
  restriction on the shores.
- For a six-vertex shore opposite a twelve-vertex shore, every
  unmarked vertex of the larger shore is at distance exactly two
  from at least one terminal
  (`no_six_twelve_shores_of_hidden_nonport`).
- In a seven-vertex shore, a high-degree nonport is universal within
  its shore, and any same-shore nonedge incident to a high-degree
  vertex is always at graph distance two.
- The auxiliary third-shell and hidden-dark degree reductions are
  graph-side statements. The arithmetic obstruction used by the
  six-plus-twelve restriction operates only on already proved
  necessary graph counts. No external solver is part of the proof.

The six-plus-twelve case with no wholly hidden nonport and the
seven-plus-eleven case are not excluded here.
