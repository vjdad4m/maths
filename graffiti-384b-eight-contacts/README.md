# Graffiti.pc #384b through eight exterior contacts

For a finite simple graph with 31 nonleaves and 31 distinct vertex triangle counts, the conjecture gives γ₂(G) ≤ |V(G)| − 16. In a possible failure the earlier reductions supply two degree-two vertices e and f with four distinct neighbors. Call these four neighbors the anchors. Count in m every other vertex of degree at least three adjacent to any anchor, once per vertex. The theorem in this package proves the inequality for m ≤ 8. It allows disconnected graphs and arbitrary order. The bound for m ≤ 7 is imported from the frozen predecessor. ## The eight-contact argument

1. The checked eight-contact theorem supplies an anchor w in exactly two triangles. One contains its ear u and the other neighbor b of u. The other contains two adjacent, private exterior vertices i and j. Every other anchor has triangle count at least 15. In the predecessor’s rank estimate, degree at least five at w would cost at least 10,205,261 and each of the three other anchors costs at least 739,024. Their sum exceeds the budget 12,407,000, so w has exactly the four neighbors u, b, i and j. [Degree bound](EightDegree.lean) · [Predecessor sources](../graffiti-384b-sparse-anchors/)
2. Each of the other three anchors sees at least four exterior vertices. With only three, its triangle count would be at most 1 + 1 + C(3,2) + 3·3 = 14. Their three neighbor sets lie among the same six contacts outside i and j, so every pair overlaps. Put the four anchors on one side, and put the ears and every exterior vertex touching at least two anchors on the other. Every fixed vertex has two opposite neighbors except w, which already has u. [Boundary proof](EightBoundary.lean)
3. The paths w–i and w–j–i have opposite parity. Either can protect w and all its internal vertices, leaving i needing just one more opposite neighbor. The same holds at j. Extend such a path through fresh exterior vertices. Meeting another fixed pin completes a cut by choosing the parity that puts its neighbor on the opposite side. Maximizing the set reachable by these two choices gives a precise alternative. The private pair then belongs to a region C closed under exterior neighbors and adjacent to no fixed pin other than w. [Two-parity extension](FlexibleTip.lean)
4. In the closed region each vertex has at least three triangles in the original graph, because the unique high score two belongs to w. Every triangle neighbor lies in C or equals w. Since w sees only i and j in C, at most one triangle at each vertex uses w. At least two triangles therefore remain wholly in C, giving internal degree at least three. A maximum cut inside C gives each vertex two opposite neighbors. Orient that cut with i opposite w and combine it with the fixed pins. This supplies w’s second neighbor. In both cases the resulting cut protects both ears, which contradicts the predecessor’s two-ear frame theorem in any failure. [Root attachment and triangle count](ClosedRegion.lean) · [Assembly](FinishEight.lean)

The previous bound m ≤ 14 leaves m = 9 through 14 in this four-anchor profile. Other profiles of the full conjecture are outside this result. The graph argument reuses the repository’s rank account, two-ear profile, link triangle budget, and protected maximum-cut theorem.
