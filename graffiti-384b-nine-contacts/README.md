# Graffiti.pc #384b through nine exterior contacts

Let G be a finite simple graph with 31 nonleaves and 31 distinct values of the number of triangles through a vertex. An ear here means a vertex of degree two. Let A be the union of the ears’ neighborhoods, and let m count vertices of degree at least three outside A having a neighbor in A. The theorem proves that m ≤ 9 implies γ₂(G) ≤ |V(G)| − 16. It includes disconnected graphs and requires no order hypothesis. The preceding reduction supplies exactly two ears with disjoint neighbor pairs in any failure. The remaining range for this profile is 10 ≤ m ≤ 14. ## The nine-contact proof

In a putative failure the sparse frontier supplies an anchor w whose exterior neighbors touch only w among the anchors. Those neighbors induce a matching and isolated vertices. Write x for their number. At m = 9, the earlier restrictions give 2 ≤ x ≤ 4.

* **Four private neighbors.** The other three anchors see at most five exterior vertices each. Their triangle counts are at most 2 + C(5,2) + 2·5 = 22. Since the three counts are distinct, at least one is at most 20. The degree of w is at least five and its triangle count is at most four. In the checked eleven-vertex sampling bound, the four anchors consequently cost at least 7,845,024 + 2,116,296 + 2·1,293,292 = 12,547,904, exceeding the available 12,407,000. The same calculation shows that every private matching row with 4 ≤ x ≤ 7 needs at least six exterior contacts outside the row. [Rank proof](NineRanks.lean)
* **Three private neighbors.** The small-row link bound gives triangle count exactly two at w. The frozen degree bound then gives degree at most four. The three private neighbors and the ear exhaust its neighborhood. The ear has both neighbors in A, so it contributes no edge to the link induced on these four vertices. The remaining three induce at most one matching edge. Thus w could lie in at most one triangle. [Local obstruction](NineTriangle.lean)
* **Two private neighbors.** The triangle count is again two. The two triangles must be the one with the incident ear and its other anchor, and the one with the two private exterior vertices. Each other anchor has a score of at least 15 and needs at least four exterior neighbors. Their neighbor sets now lie in a common set of seven, so every pair meets. Placing the ears and shared contacts opposite the anchors protects all the fixed vertices except w. The private triangle gives both possible path parities from w. The frozen flexible-region and internal maximum-cut lemmas then supply w’s second opposite neighbor. The resulting cut protects both ears. [Triangle geometry](NineTriangle.lean) · [Shared-contact proof](NineBoundary.lean) · [Original-graph conclusion](FinishNine.lean)

The m ≤ 8 theorem is an imported premise only in that range. At nine, the proof reuses the earlier matching-row frontier, triangle-link counts, rank account, score-two degree bound, and cut lemmas. The boundary and assembly arguments are adapted from the eight-contact proof to work under the newly derived hypotheses.
