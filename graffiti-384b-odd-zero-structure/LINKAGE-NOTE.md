# Proposed continuation for disjoint neighborhoods

This note is a mathematical proof candidate. None of its linkage or parity statements are counted among the Lean results.

Let z be a degree-two vertex of triangle count zero and e a count-one ear in a hypothetical failure. Work in J consisting of triangle edges and the two edges at z. The checked deletion theorem gives an e–z path avoiding any prescribed third vertex. Suppose the two neighbors a,b of e and the two neighbors c,d of z are all distinct. In this graph a,b are adjacent and c,d are nonadjacent.

1. Two internally disjoint e–z paths would yield disjoint arms from a,b to c,d avoiding e,z, after interchanging c,d if necessary. The two-path conclusion here is the vertex form of Menger's theorem. A local Lean theorem with the required hypotheses or a direct proof still needs to be supplied.
2. Choose such a linkage minimizing the sum of its lengths over both pairings. Write the arm lengths as k,l and their last edges as u–c and v–d. Both lengths are positive because the four terminals are distinct. If k+l is even, closing the arms via e,z gives an even cycle protecting both designated vertices.
3. Suppose k+l is odd. The edge u–c lies in a triangle with a third vertex w. The choice of c and the disjointness of the arms exclude e,z as w. If w is outside the other arm, the usual parity detour on the reversed c-arm changes the parity of this arm, keeping the linkage disjoint. If w lies on the c-arm itself, take its suffix toward a and choose the one-edge or two-edge prefix from c to w with the required parity. For an arm of length one its triangle witness is outside the arm, so insertion always works. Apply the same argument to v–d.
4. If neither detour is available, take a witness on the other arm for each terminal edge. Let i be the position on the a-arm of the witness for v–d, and let j be the position on the b-arm of the witness for u–c. The inequality c≁d gives i≤k−1 and j≤l−1. Keeping the two prefixes and linking them to d,c respectively gives total length i+j+2. Minimality therefore gives i=k−1 and j=l−1. Thus u,v are adjacent and both see c and d.
5. Color alternately along the two prefixes from e through a,b up to u,v. Their lengths from e are k,l, so the final colors differ. Give z the color of u and give c,d the color of v. Each of z,u sees c,d across, and each of c,d sees z,u across. Vertex v sees u and its predecessor across; that predecessor is e if b=v and is distinct from u in either case. All other vertices pinned on the prefixes have two crossing path edges. Maximize the cut on the remaining vertices to cover all vertices of degree at least three as well. This would contradict the equality statement for the positive representatives together with z.

The argument concerns only the degree-two-zero case. Zero leaves or isolates require a separate argument. The local obstruction on two triangular ears gives a reason to keep the nonadjacency of c,d explicit.
