# Graffiti.pc #384b at 27 high vertices with a low neighbor of a degree-two vertex

Let a finite simple graph have order n, exactly 29 distinct vertex triangle counts, and exactly 27 vertices of degree at least three. Let an ear e have neighbors w and v, where w has degree at least three and v has degree at most two. Write X for the 26 high vertices other than w. These hypotheses cover a pendant triangle, a leaf next to the ear, and two adjacent ears with different high neighbors. Connectivity is optional. The sufficient conditions below give **γ₂(G) ≤ n − 15**. [General attachment theorem](PendantBounds.lean) · [Third-ear theorem](ThirdEar.lean)

Let m count the vertices in X with at least one neighbor outside X. A degree-d condition in the table means either an exposed vertex of X has degree at most d, or two adjacent vertices of X both have degree at most d. The edge endpoints can have neighbors outside X. The entries give the least m certified by the corresponding row.

| Nonleaf hypothesis | Minimum degree in X | No additional condition | Degree-5 condition | Degree-4 condition |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Any | 3 | 10 | 9 | 8 |
| Any | 4 | 8 | 7 | 5 |
| Third ear available | 3 | 8 | 6 | 4 |
| Third ear available | 4 | 4 | 2 | 1 |
| Any | 5 | 0 | 0 | 0 |

The third ear is available when there are at least 30 nonleaves, or at least 29 nonleaves and v is a leaf. In both cases it can be chosen distinct from e and v. For example, at 30 nonleaves, if X has minimum degree four and contains two adjacent vertices of degree four, the conjectured inequality holds with no separately imposed exposure condition. A retained high root has at least two incident triangles. If all its neighbors were low, those triangles would require four degree-two vertices, but there are only three. A high neighbor of the root therefore supplies the single required exposure. An exposed quartic gives the same conclusion. [Theorem thirty_quartic](ThirdEar.lean)

The catalogue counts each distinct number of incident unordered triangles once, including zero when realized. A vertex outside a 2-dominating set needs two distinct neighbors inside. The earlier 29-type theorem left two above-minimum nonleaf profiles, 29 and 30, both with 27 high vertices. The previous pendant proof gave the thresholds 14, 12, and 1 for minimum degrees three, four, and five, with the last exposure automatic at these two quotas. ## How the savings combine

Sample ten of the 26 vertices of X. Initially retain the sample and the exterior of X, then omit the ear e. Each high vertex has a distinct triangle count of at least two. At zero-based score rank i its count is therefore at least i + 2. If i + 2 equals the upper bound C(d,2) at its least possible degree d, the vertex either has degree at least d + 1, reducing its deficiency charge, or has degree d and a clique neighborhood. In the second case the proof can remove the sampled vertex when two of its neighbors are retained. All such removals can occur simultaneously. Different removable vertices are nonadjacent, and any vertex adjacent to one outside the remaining seeds has two surviving neighbors. Every previously deficient pool vertex keeps its original number of seed neighbors. [Simultaneous removals](CleanTrial.lean) · [Rank-by-rank counting](ActiveRank.lean)

For an internal edge xy whose endpoints have no exterior neighbors, restrict to samples missing x and y. The earlier four-class formula counts exactly the trials in which x has one retained neighbor and y is deficient. The minimum over all admissible local neighborhood intersections is at least **251,680** if both degrees lie in 3–5, and **539,682** if both lie in 3–4. Keeping y then allows x to be omitted. The rank calculation also measures the lower deficiency charge at an exposed vertex. A low endpoint with an exterior neighbor already pays the required allowance, so the graph-level result applies to arbitrary low-degree edges inside X. [Edge counts](InternalEdge.lean) · [Selection of events](PendantBounds.lean)

For the third ear z, choose two vertices of X including every neighbor of z in X. There are C(24,8) = **735,471** trials containing both. On these trials every neighbor of z is retained. A sampled high vertex removed for its clique neighborhood cannot be adjacent to a degree-two vertex. Removing z therefore leaves the deficient set and its seed-neighbor counts intact. An eligible interior edge can still earn its separate saving on that same trial. [Third-ear count and joint repair](ThirdEar.lean)

The weighted theorem keeps individual exterior credits. Let M be the sum, over all exposed vertices, of 591,790 at degrees three or four, 381,840 at degree five, 171,890 at degree six, and zero at higher degrees. Let I count any family of trials with a usable interior edge, counted at most once per trial. The two-ear estimate suffices when 290,000m + M + I exceeds 2,785,582 at floor three, or 2,150,887 at floor four. In particular four exposed vertices of degree at most four suffice at floor three, and three such vertices suffice at floor four. With the extra ear, the sufficient thresholds for 170,000m + M + I are 1,211,301 and 576,606 respectively. The table also uses the smaller exterior price 170,000 on the ordinary floor-four branch. All tabulations use kernel reduction on local integer bounds. [Weighted theorem and small-contact corollary](WeightedExposure.lean) · [Exact three-ear budget](ThirdEar.lean)

## Reuse and remaining scope

The proof adapts the active-vertex trial and insertion count in the published 31-type SimplicialTrial proof. Its original fiber fixes a pool of size 27. Here the pool has size 26 and the rank proof uses the alternative between a clique neighborhood and an extra unit of degree. The frozen sources supply the finite-range and boundary charges, rank summation, clique characterization, fixed-anchor four-class incidence formula, pair repair, and batch repair. This result adds a simultaneous rank and deficient-edge saving; the third-ear proof preserves both. [Published simplicial trial](https://github.com/vjdad4m/maths/blob/d24cf4b9f561a8e737a9abbe47faf52d22aba0d1/graffiti-384b-independent13/SimplicialTrial.lean) · [Earlier repair constructions](https://github.com/vjdad4m/maths/tree/7fa1027f60cf9f7f29df69071dbc15eb159bfbf9/graffiti-384b-discounted-repairs)

The unrestricted 27-high case remains open. For a pendant failure at 29 nonleaves the exposed count must now be at most nine. At 30 nonleaves it must be at most seven; with minimum degree four in X it must be at most three and X can contain neither an exposed quartic nor an edge joining two quartics. Small exposure alone can still leave the rank inequality short. The results also leave other attachment geometries open.
